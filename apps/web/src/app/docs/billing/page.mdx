# Billing

Billing belongs to the **hosted service at sendsprite.com**. On an instance you
host yourself `BILLING_ENABLED` defaults to `false`, and with it off there is no
Billing page, no checkout, no provider webhook endpoint, no billing job and no
plan-derived limit — sending behaves exactly as it did before the feature
existed. If you never turn the flag on, none of this page applies to you; if you
are thinking of turning it on, read **Turning billing on** in
[Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting) first, because the flip is not free.

## What you are paying for

Sendsprite sends through **your own Amazon SES account** — the one you connect
in **Dashboard → Settings → Instance**. AWS bills you for delivery at its own
rates, directly, and Sendsprite never marks it up or resells it. There is no
per-email cost on our side to recover.

What the plans pay for is the control plane: the API, the dashboard, domain
provisioning and verification, the event pipeline, webhook delivery, tracking,
the SMTP relay, the CLI and the MCP server.

## Plans

Three tiers — **Free**, **Pro** and **Scale** — each with a fixed monthly price
and an included volume of emails, and the two paid tiers with a per-1,000
overage rate beyond it. The current prices and volumes are in **Dashboard →
Settings → Billing**, which reads them from the payment provider's own
catalogue.

They are deliberately not repeated here. No price, no product id and no
allowance is compiled into Sendsprite: each product carries `plan`,
`included_emails` and `overage_per_1k_cents` in its provider-side metadata, the
app resolves a subscription to entitlements by reading those three fields, and a
price change in the provider dashboard therefore takes effect without a deploy.
A table on this page would start drifting the day it was written.

"Emails" means messages accepted by the Sendsprite API, the SMTP relay or the
dashboard. A message refused before it is queued — an unverified domain, a
suppressed recipient, a validation error — is never counted. A message that
later bounces **is** counted: it was sent. Recipients do not multiply the count:
one API call with ten `to` addresses is one email.

## Included volume, overage and limits

On a plan with **no metered price** — Free is one — the included volume is a
hard limit. Past it the API answers `429 monthly_quota_exceeded` until the
period rolls over.

On a plan **with** a metered price, sending past the included volume is billed
at the overage rate rather than blocked: losing your sending because you had a
good week is the wrong failure. Which of the two a subscription gets is read off
the subscription itself, not off a flag in our code.

Either way every response from the emails endpoints carries the usual headers:

```
x-ratelimit-limit: 50000
x-ratelimit-remaining: 48211
x-ratelimit-reset: 1789084800
```

`x-ratelimit-reset` is the end of your billing period as a Unix timestamp.
`x-ratelimit-limit` reads `unlimited` on a plan whose overage is billed, because
there is no monthly ceiling left to report.

### The overage spend ceiling

The paid tiers carry a **ceiling on overage charges** per cycle (`cap_amount` on
the metered price — $200 on Pro, $500 on Scale). It exists so that a runaway
loop cannot turn into a five-figure invoice.

**Reaching it does not stop your sending.** That is deliberate, and it is the
opposite of what the word "cap" usually suggests: the ceiling bounds the _bill_,
not the _service_. Past it you keep sending and we stop charging for the excess.
Nothing in Sendsprite enforces it either — it is provider configuration, applied
when the invoice is drawn, and no code path in this app reads it except to
display it.

If you want sending itself to stop at a number, ask for
`team_settings.monthly_limit` to be set: that is a hard cap and it takes
precedence over the plan.

## Your billing period

Your period runs from the day you subscribed, not from the 1st: subscribe on the
10th and your allowance renews on the 10th. The usage figure, the meter on the
billing page and `x-ratelimit-reset` all follow that **billing** period — the
calendar month is not involved.

Free teams have no subscription and therefore no anniversary, so their window is
the UTC calendar month.

If a renewal has not been confirmed by the provider yet, the stored period is
rolled forward by its own length so there is always a window in force. That
keeps the anniversary you bought rather than jumping the boundary to the 1st;
for a subscription billed near the end of a month it can drift by a day or so
while the renewal is late, and it settles as soon as the renewal lands.

## How usage is measured

Usage is rolled up **hourly**. Once an hour has closed and settled for 30
minutes, Sendsprite sends one usage record for it to the payment provider.
Nothing is sent per email, so a slow or unavailable payment provider can never
slow down or fail a send — the worst case is that the usage figure on your
invoice catches up a little later. Each record carries a deterministic id, so a
retried delivery is discarded by the provider instead of being billed twice.

**We meter on acceptance.** The unit is a message the API accepted, not a
message SES delivered. Once an hour has been reported, that hour is final: an
email that is cancelled or fails more than about 30 minutes after its hour
closed has already been counted, and nothing un-counts it. In practice a send
resolves within minutes of being accepted, so the window is narrow — but it is
real, and this is the honest description of what you are billed for.

The number on your billing page is read live from your email log, so it is
always current; the "reported to the payment provider" figure beside it is how
much has reached the provider so far, and it lags by up to about ninety minutes.

## Upgrading, downgrading and payment methods

**Dashboard → Settings → Billing**:

- **Choose** on a plan opens a checkout for it. A change takes effect once the
  payment clears and the provider's webhook lands.
- **Manage billing** opens the customer portal: payment methods, invoices,
  receipts and cancellation.

Owners and admins can change the plan (`billing.manage`); members see the plan
and the usage but get no buttons.

Cancelling keeps your plan until the end of the period you have paid for; after
that the team returns to the Free plan's limits.

## When a payment fails

The provider marks the subscription `past_due` and retries the charge. Your plan
keeps working for **7 days** from that moment, and the billing page shows an
amber banner carrying the deadline.

Past those 7 days the team is held to the **Free plan's allowance**. The
subscription stays open, the portal keeps working, and the paid caps come back
the moment a charge succeeds. Sending is not cut off the hour a card expires,
and nothing is deleted.

## Self-hosting

With `BILLING_ENABLED=false` (the default):

- no Billing page, and no Billing card under Settings;
- `POST /api/billing/webhook` returns 404;
- no billing job is registered with the worker, and no payment SDK is loaded;
- the only send limits are the ones you set yourself
  (`team_settings.daily_limit` / `monthly_limit`) and your SES account's own
  quota.

Turning it on needs `POLAR_ACCESS_TOKEN`, `POLAR_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `POLAR_SERVER`
and a webhook endpoint registered with the provider at
`https://your-instance/api/billing/webhook`, plus the products, the meter and
the metered prices set up on the provider side. The runbook — including the
grandfathering step that keeps the flip from capping every existing team — is
under **Turning billing on** in [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting).

Even on the hosted service, `team_settings` wins over the plan wherever it is
set — per team and per column, so one team's monthly cap can be lifted without
touching its plan. It is the operator's escape hatch.

## Payments and invoicing

Payments on the hosted service are processed by **Polar**, acting as merchant of
record: Polar is the seller on your invoice and handles VAT and sales tax.
Invoices and receipts live in the customer portal.

Your Sendsprite **team id** is the customer reference passed to Polar, so it
appears on the customer record there. Note that team ids carry no prefix, unlike
every other public Sendsprite id (`em_`, `dom_`, `key_`, `wh_`) — they come from
the authentication provider.
